\begin{abstract}
Per-pixel field-boundary segmentation models produce probability maps, but downstream users need polygons, and the conversion step is where accuracy is won or lost. We study that conversion for a frozen UNet with an EfficientNet-B7 encoder, retraining nothing, and improve only how its per-pixel field and boundary probabilities become field polygons. We evaluate with polygon-level metrics, led by Panoptic Quality (PQ = SQ $\times$ RQ), on the Fields of The World benchmark~\cite{kerner2024ftw}, a smallholder subset, and across Sentinel-2 and PlanetScope imagery. Our central result is that a single watershed threshold is the wrong abstraction: FieldVote, a threshold-consensus rule that runs watershed at many h-minima thresholds and takes a per-pixel majority vote, reaches a macro PQ of 0.325 over eleven held-out countries. This is within 0.001 of the trained-model benchmark headline of 0.324, obtained with a frozen model and no retraining, and it improves over the single best per-country threshold (0.264) by a statistically significant margin (Wilcoxon $p = 0.049$). The vote also yields a label-free per-field confidence score that predicts per-field accuracy within a region. Applied to the global Fields of The World Sentinel-2 prediction store, the consensus runs unchanged across sensors and continents, which to our knowledge is the first global-scale evaluation of a field-boundary postprocessing technique, with the agreement signal indicating where it helps. We report negative results as well. Geometric refinement designed for buildings does not help organic field boundaries: regularization and corner smoothing each move mean PQ by at most 0.002 over 23 countries, and the regularization gain shows no significant tie to field rectangularity ($r = 0.135$, $p = 0.55$). Douglas-Peucker simplification is near-free compression, cutting vertices per polygon sixteen-fold while PQ falls only 0.005. Threshold consensus is the only postprocessing lever we found that moves the metric.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Field boundaries are the unit of analysis for crop monitoring, yield estimation, subsidy administration, and land-use accounting. Mapping them at continental scale requires automated delineation from satellite imagery. The dominant approach trains a convolutional network to predict, for every pixel, the probability of field interior and of field boundary~\cite{kerner2024ftw, waldner2020decode}. These probability maps are accurate and well studied. They are also not what a user needs: a cadastre, a yield model, and a subsidy auditor consume vector polygons, not rasters of class scores. The step that converts probabilities into polygons therefore determines the quality of every downstream product, and it is the step we study here.

Pixel-level metrics such as intersection over union reward correct labeling of large interior regions and are nearly insensitive to whether two adjacent fields are correctly separated. The object that matters is the field, so the metric that matters is object-level. We lead with Panoptic Quality~\cite{kirillov2019panoptic}, which factors as segmentation quality times recognition quality (PQ = SQ $\times$ RQ) and penalizes both merged and fragmented fields, complemented by Boundary IoU~\cite{cheng2021boundaryiou} and average precision. Under these metrics a model with excellent pixel accuracy can still produce unusable polygons. Improving vectorization, rather than the network, is the highest-leverage and least-explored part of the pipeline.

Converting a boundary probability map into polygons is classically done by watershed segmentation seeded at suppressed minima~\cite{soille2004morphological, koyuncu2016iterative, waldner2020decode}. The result depends sharply on the h-minima suppression threshold: a low threshold over-fragments large fields, a high threshold merges small ones, and no single value is correct across countries. Optimal values for the large rectangular fields of northern Europe over-fragment the small irregular fields of smallholder regions, and the reverse merges large fields into blobs. Field geometry compounds the difficulty, since agricultural parcels are organic rather than rectilinear, so geometric priors imported from building extraction~\cite{zorzi2022polyworld, girard2021framefield, zorzi2020regularization} do not transfer. Finally, ground-truth polygons are expensive, so any improvement must require no per-region labels for tuning.

We change nothing about the network. We treat its frozen probability maps as fixed inputs and study only the conversion to polygons, which isolates the contribution of postprocessing and keeps the comparison against the trained-model headline fair. Our main finding is that selecting a single watershed threshold is the wrong abstraction. Instead, we run watershed at many thresholds and take a per-pixel majority vote over the resulting segmentations. Consensus reaches a macro PQ of 0.325 over eleven held-out countries, within 0.001 of the trained-model benchmark headline of 0.324, while the single best per-country threshold reaches 0.264 and the DelineateAnything baseline~\cite{lavreniuk2025delineateanything} reaches 0.046 (0.133 in its strongest configuration). The improvement over the single best threshold is statistically significant (Wilcoxon $p = 0.049$, bootstrap 95\% confidence interval on the mean gain $[0.012, 0.112]$). Consensus wins decisively where a single threshold over-fragments, for example Belgium (0.529 versus 0.306) and Croatia (0.358 versus 0.205), and loses on the smallest-field smallholder cases (Cambodia, Portugal) and Germany. The same vote agreement yields a label-free per-field confidence score.

We also report negative results, because they redirect effort away from dead ends. Geometric refinement built for buildings does not help organic fields, and soft-boundary watershed is neutral. Simplification is near-free compression rather than an accuracy lever. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Threshold consensus (FieldVote)}, a per-pixel majority vote over watershed segmentations run at many h-minima thresholds, which reaches macro PQ 0.325 on eleven held-out countries, within 0.001 of the trained-model headline with a frozen model and a statistically significant gain over the single best threshold, plus a size-adaptive variant that further improves the macro by protecting small fields.
\item \textbf{A label-free per-field confidence} from threshold-vote agreement that, within a region, predicts per-field IoU against ground truth (Spearman 0.20 to 0.42, $p < 0.0001$, in 5 of 6 countries), requiring no labels.
\item \textbf{A systematic negative-result study of geometric postprocessing}, showing that building-derived regularization, smoothing, and raster cleanup do not transfer to organic fields, that simplification is near-free sixteen-fold vertex compression, and that soft-boundary watershed is neutral.
\item \textbf{A global, cross-sensor evaluation.} We run the consensus unchanged on the global Fields of The World Sentinel-2 prediction store across eight regions on five continents, to our knowledge the first global-scale evaluation of a field-boundary postprocessing technique, and show the agreement signal indicates where it helps.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Field-boundary delineation and watershed postprocessing.}
Field delineation is commonly cast as dense prediction of a field and boundary map followed by instance extraction. Waldner and Diakogiannis~\cite{waldner2020decode} predict extent, boundary, and distance with a FracTAL ResUNet and recover parcels by seeded watershed, establishing the encoder-then-watershed pipeline that the Fields of The World benchmark~\cite{kerner2024ftw} adopts. The watershed is classical morphology~\cite{soille2004morphological}: flooding a surface from markers, where marker choice governs whether parcels merge or fragment. Koyuncu et al.~\cite{koyuncu2016iterative} make marker selection explicit by sweeping the h-minima depth and iterating, which exposes over-segmentation and under-segmentation as a single tunable axis. Wagner and Oppelt~\cite{wagner2020growing} drive a hierarchical watershed from learned contours under Sentinel-2 resolution. These methods fix one operating point per scene; we retain the full h-minima sweep and combine its outputs.

\paragraph{Learned versus geometric polygon extraction.}
A second line extracts polygons directly. Girard et al.~\cite{girard2021framefield} learn a frame field aligned to parcel geometry and report that downstream Douglas-Peucker simplification lowers AP. PolyWorld~\cite{zorzi2022polyworld} predicts vertices and connectivity as a graph. Geometric refinement matured on buildings, where right-angle priors hold: Zorzi et al.~\cite{zorzi2020regularization} regularize masks with an adversarial network that imposes rectilinear outlines. We test whether these building-oriented operators transfer to organic fields and find they do not.

\paragraph{Ensembles, consensus, and uncertainty.}
Deep ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017deepensembles}, MC dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout}, and test-time augmentation~\cite{wang2019tta} aggregate multiple predictions but retrain or re-infer the network. Our method is inspired by this view but aggregates over a postprocessing hyperparameter rather than model weights: it keeps boundaries that are stable across the h-minima sweep, acting as a stability filter rather than an independent-estimator ensemble.

\paragraph{Evaluation metrics.}
We lead with object-level metrics because pixel accuracy tolerates merged or split parcels. Panoptic Quality~\cite{kirillov2019panoptic} penalizes both localization and instance-count error, and we complement it with Boundary IoU~\cite{cheng2021boundaryiou}. This object-first protocol matches recent smallholder and pan-European benchmarks~\cite{persello2023ai4smallfarms, dandrimont2023ai4boundaries} and the Fields of The World evaluation~\cite{kerner2024ftw}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
We postprocess a frozen UNet with an EfficientNet-B7 encoder that produces a per-pixel softmax over field interior, boundary, and background. We hold the weights fixed and replace only the conversion of these probabilities into polygons (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).

\subsection{Threshold-consensus polygonization}
\label{sec:consensus}
Marker-controlled watershed seeds from regional minima after an h-minima transform that suppresses minima shallower than a depth $h$~\cite{koyuncu2016iterative, waldner2020decode}. The choice of $h$ governs fragmentation, and a single $h$ fixes one point on the over-segmentation versus under-segmentation trade-off for an entire region. We run the watershed independently at each threshold in a fixed set $H = \{h_1, \dots, h_K\}$. Run $k$ yields a label map $L_k$, which we convert to a per-pixel field indicator. The consensus assigns a pixel to field when at least a fraction $\tau_v$ of the runs do,
\begin{equation}
\bar{f}(p) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{1}[p \in \text{field in } L_k] \geq \tau_v ,
\label{eq:vote}
\end{equation}
with the majority vote $\tau_v = 0.5$. We polygonize the connected components of the consensus mask. Equation~\eqref{eq:vote} keeps a field only where it is stable across the sweep: spurious splits at a single shallow $h$ are voted down, and divisions that recur across the range are retained. The procedure adds no learned parameters.

\subsection{Geometric refinement and raster cleanup}
\label{sec:geometric}
We test whether refinement operators developed for buildings transfer to fields, applying four stages to the polygon output: Douglas-Peucker simplification; rectilinear edge regularization that snaps edges toward dominant orientations~\cite{zorzi2020regularization}; Chaikin corner-cutting smoothing; and a pre-vectorization morphological raster cleanup (small-component removal and hole filling) applied before the watershed.

\subsection{Threshold-agreement confidence}
\label{sec:confidence}
The sweep supplies a label-free confidence at no extra cost. For a consensus field $F$ we define its agreement as the fraction of the $K$ runs whose label map contains a basin overlapping $F$ with intersection over union at least $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
a(F) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{1}\!\left[\exists\, c \in L_k : \mathrm{IoU}(c, F) \geq \theta\right].
\label{eq:agree}
\end{equation}
A field delineated identically at every threshold scores $a(F)=1$. Because $a(F)$ needs no ground truth, it is available wherever the model runs.

\subsection{Size-adaptive consensus}
\label{sec:adaptive}
The uniform majority vote helps where fields are large and clean but can over-merge where they are small and dense. We therefore choose the rule per patch using a label-free signal, the mean area of the vote-consensus instances: below a size threshold $T$ we blend the vote with the field probability ($0.4\,\bar{f} + 0.6\,p_{\text{field}} \geq 0.5$), otherwise we keep the pure vote. The switch needs no labels and adds one parameter $T$.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
We postprocess one frozen UNet with an EfficientNet-B7 encoder, trained on Fields of The World~\cite{kerner2024ftw}, and operate only on its stored softmax so every comparison isolates the polygonization step. We report two scopes. The headline polygon-quality comparison uses the eleven held-out countries (\textsc{Heldout-11}) on which the benchmark reports its macro. The geometric-refinement study uses the 23 countries for which the full polygon stack was scored, and the confidence and soft-boundary studies use the subset for which per-pixel probabilities were stored; these counts reflect data availability, not selection. Baselines are the benchmark trained-model headline~\cite{kerner2024ftw}, a single best per-country h-minima threshold, and DelineateAnything~\cite{lavreniuk2025delineateanything} run on the same imagery in both its default and strongest configurations. Metrics are Panoptic Quality with its SQ and RQ factors~\cite{kirillov2019panoptic}, average precision, Boundary IoU~\cite{cheng2021boundaryiou}, and mean vertex count; all macro numbers are averaged across the relevant countries.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Consensus matches the trained headline}
Table~\ref{tab:headline} reports macro PQ on \textsc{Heldout-11}. Threshold consensus reaches 0.325, within 0.001 of the benchmark trained-model headline of 0.324, using the frozen model with no retraining, and improves on the single best per-country threshold (0.264) by 0.061. This gain is statistically significant across countries (Wilcoxon signed-rank $p = 0.049$; bootstrap 95\% confidence interval on the mean gain $[0.012, 0.112]$). DelineateAnything reaches 0.046 by default and 0.133 in its strongest configuration, well below both. We do not claim statistical equivalence with the benchmark; we report that the gap is 0.001 and that consensus recovers the trained-model polygon quality purely by aggregating operating points of the existing softmax. The gain is not limited to PQ: macro Boundary IoU~\cite{cheng2021boundaryiou} also rises, from 0.142 for the single best threshold to 0.159 for consensus, so the vote improves edge fidelity as well as instance count.

\subsection{The vote threshold is robust}
Figure~\ref{fig:vote} sweeps the vote fraction $\tau_v$ from 0.3 to 0.7. Macro PQ is flat near the majority vote, 0.327 at 0.3 and 0.4, 0.325 at 0.5, and declines only past 0.6. The majority vote is therefore a principled and near-optimal operating point, not a tuned one.

\subsection{A size-adaptive rule recovers smallholder accuracy}
The blend of vote and field probability helps small or fragmented fields but slightly hurts large clean ones, so on its own it ties the uniform vote. Choosing between them per patch by field size (Section~\ref{sec:adaptive}) captures both. On a controlled softmax-derived pipeline the size-adaptive rule raises macro PQ from 0.304 for the uniform vote to 0.320 (with $T$ between 50{,}000 and 100{,}000 m\textsuperscript{2}), recovering the gains on the smallholder and over-fragmenting countries (Belgium, Croatia, Slovenia, Lithuania) without losing the large-field countries (Germany, South Africa, Sweden), as Figure~\ref{fig:adaptive} shows.

\subsection{Geometric refinement does not help organic fields}
Refinements designed for built structures do not transfer (Table~\ref{tab:refine}). Over 23 countries, raw polygons score 0.265, Douglas-Peucker simplification 0.264, building-style regularization 0.263, and Chaikin smoothing 0.263. Regularization improves only 1 of 23 countries, and pre-vectorization raster cleanup improves 3 of 25. The per-country regularization change shows no significant association with field rectangularity (Pearson $r = 0.135$, $p = 0.55$, $n = 22$), which is consistent with rectilinear priors offering no useful signal for organic fields.

\subsection{Simplification is near-free compression}
Although simplification does not raise PQ, it compresses geometry at almost no cost (Fig.~\ref{fig:simplify}). As the Douglas-Peucker tolerance grows to 6 m, mean vertices per polygon drop from 188 to 12, a sixteen-fold reduction, while PQ falls only from 0.366 to 0.361. Simplification is worth applying for storage and downstream efficiency, and the choice is orthogonal to the consensus gain.

\subsection{Soft-boundary watershed is neutral; agreement calibrates per-field quality}
Thresholding the boundary-class softmax with a swept cut, instead of taking the argmax, changes mean PQ by less than 0.001 across 9 countries and helps 4 of 9, so it is not a useful lever. The threshold-agreement confidence of Section~\ref{sec:confidence} is more informative. Within a country, higher-agreement fields are more accurate: the per-field Spearman correlation between agreement and IoU against ground truth is positive and significant in 5 of the 6 countries tested, from 0.20 (Cambodia) to 0.42 (Slovenia), all $p < 0.0001$, with only the smallest test set not significant (Rwanda, 174 fields). Across countries the signal does not rank regions by accuracy: mean agreement and macro PQ are uncorrelated ($r = 0.07$). Threshold agreement is therefore a within-region quality estimate rather than a cross-region one, and because it needs no labels it flags unreliable fields at inference time wherever the model runs.

\subsection{Global, cross-sensor demonstration}
\label{sec:global}
We apply the consensus to the global Fields of The World Sentinel-2 prediction store, a 10\,m global mosaic of the same three-class softmax. The model that produced those predictions is Sentinel-2 based, so this also tests whether the consensus, which we developed on a Planet model, transfers across sensors. To our knowledge this is the first evaluation of a field-boundary postprocessing technique at global scale. We process windows spanning eight agricultural regions on five continents (Table~\ref{tab:global}); the consensus runs unchanged on the Sentinel-2 store. Its effect is governed by the same label-free agreement signal. In high-agreement regions (Argentina, France, Australia, Brazil; agreement 0.95 to 0.99) it de-fragments, for example Argentina from 426 to 172 polygons (Fig.~\ref{fig:global}). In low-agreement regions (Punjab, Heilongjiang, Ethiopia; agreement 0.16 to 0.42) the predictions are themselves unstable across thresholds, and the consensus produces more and smaller polygons. Agreement therefore serves as a deployment indicator for when to apply the consensus. We do not report polygon-level accuracy here, because global ground-truth parcels are not available; quantifying global accuracy against reference data is future work.

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{Why consensus works.}
The optimal h-minima depth is not a global constant; it shifts with field size, parcel geometry, and boundary contrast. Choosing a single threshold commits to one point on a sharp trade-off, and the wrong point costs PQ. The consensus does not select the threshold, it keeps the field boundaries that recur across the sweep, so splits that appear only at shallow depths and merges that appear only at deep depths are filtered out. The gain is largest where a single threshold over-fragments, Belgium (0.306 to 0.529) and Croatia (0.205 to 0.358), the regions whose optimum lies farthest from the global choice. For this model and benchmark, a frozen model with better polygonization recovers the trained-model headline; we do not claim this holds for every architecture.

\paragraph{Why refinement and soft boundaries do not.}
Building regularizers and frame-field polygonizers encode a rectilinear prior~\cite{girard2021framefield, zorzi2022polyworld, zorzi2020regularization} that is correct for buildings and wrong for fields shaped by terrain, hydrology, and incremental division. The absence of a significant rectangularity association is consistent with the prior carrying no useful information for organic boundaries. Simplification is the one geometric operation worth keeping, for compression rather than accuracy. Soft-boundary watershed is neutral because the boundary information a soft cut exposes is already captured by the consensus over depths.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Consensus is not uniformly better. It loses on the smallest-field smallholder cases, Cambodia (0.171 to 0.095) and Portugal (0.129 to 0.097), and on Germany (0.325 to 0.312); when fields approach the resolution limit, averaging over thresholds blurs already-fragile boundaries~\cite{persello2023ai4smallfarms, dandrimont2023ai4boundaries}. The headline gain is a property of medium-to-large parcel landscapes. We postprocess one frozen model, so we do not separate errors that postprocessing can fix from errors baked into the probability map, and PQ can mask systematic boundary displacement that contour metrics would expose~\cite{avbelj2015polis}.

\section{Conclusion}
We treat field-boundary delineation as a polygonization problem on a frozen segmentation model and show that the conversion from probabilities to polygons, not the model, is where accuracy is won or lost. Threshold consensus, a per-pixel majority vote over watershed segmentations run at many h-minima depths, raises held-out macro PQ from 0.264 to 0.325, within 0.001 of the trained-model benchmark headline, with no retraining and a statistically significant gain over the single best threshold. The same experiments rule out the obvious alternatives: building-derived geometric refinement does not transfer to organic fields, and soft-boundary watershed is neutral. Simplification earns its place as sixteen-fold near-free vertex compression. The vote agreement additionally provides label-free per-field confidence and, applied to the global Sentinel-2 prediction store, indicates where the consensus transfers, the first global-scale evaluation of such a technique. The method fails on the smallest smallholder fields, which marks the direction for future work: a size-adaptive consensus that protects small parcels while retaining the variance reduction that large fields enjoy.
