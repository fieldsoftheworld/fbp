\title{Postprocessing Matters: Inference-Time Polygon Refinement for Agricultural Field-Boundary Delineation}

\author{Aviskar Giri \and Isaac Corley}
\institute{Taylor Geospatial Institute, St.\ Louis, MO, USA\\
\email{aviskar.giri@taylorgeospatial.org}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Semantic segmentation models for agricultural field-boundary mapping produce accurate pixel masks, yet the polygons derived from those masks are the deliverable that downstream users consume, and the conversion from mask to polygon is rarely evaluated or optimized.
We study this conversion as a first-class problem: given a frozen segmentation model, we improve the extraction of field polygons from its predictions and measure the effect with polygon-level metrics.
A threshold-consensus polygonizer, which runs watershed at multiple seed thresholds and combines the outputs by per-pixel majority vote, recovers polygon-level accuracy comparable to the model headline without retraining, and the gain concentrates on regions whose single-threshold output over-fragments.
In contrast, geometric refinement operations developed for building footprints, namely edge regularization and boundary smoothing, do not improve and slightly reduce polygon-level accuracy on organic field boundaries, an effect that weakens as fields become more rectilinear.
We also derive a label-free per-field confidence signal from threshold agreement.
We evaluate across Sentinel-2 and PlanetScope imagery with a focus on smallholder regions.
\keywords{Field boundary delineation \and Postprocessing \and Instance segmentation \and Watershed \and Remote sensing.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Mapping the boundaries of agricultural fields supports crop monitoring, yield estimation, and food-security planning, and it is the prerequisite for parcel-level analysis in regions that lack cadastral records~\cite{kerner2024ftw,persello2023ai4smallfarms}.
Recent benchmarks frame the task as segmentation of satellite imagery followed by extraction of per-field polygons~\cite{kerner2024ftw,waldner2020decode}.
Reported progress is dominated by improvements to the model: architectures, losses, and training data.
The step that turns a predicted mask into polygons, by contrast, receives little attention and is seldom evaluated with the metrics that matter for the polygons themselves.

This gap has practical consequences.
A high pixel-IoU mask can yield polygons that over-fragment a single field into many parts, merge adjacent fields, or carry jagged boundaries that no downstream system can use~\cite{cheng2021boundaryiou,girard2021framefield}.
Improving the model is expensive and, once a model is deployed at scale, often infeasible.
Postprocessing is cheap, runs once, and applies to any existing prediction product.

\paragraph{Problem statement.}
Given a frozen segmentation model that outputs per-pixel field and boundary probabilities, inference-time postprocessing recovers polygon-level accuracy comparable to retraining, the recovery generalizes across Sentinel-2 and PlanetScope imagery, and it applies at global scale.
This claim is falsifiable: we measure polygon-level accuracy before and after postprocessing and compare against the model headline and against the strongest baseline polygonizer.

\paragraph{Why it is hard.}
The optimal watershed seed threshold varies by more than an order of magnitude across regions, so a single fixed setting is not adequate~\cite{waldner2020decode}.
Geometric refinement that improves building footprints, which are predominantly rectilinear, may distort organic field boundaries~\cite{zorzi2022polyworld}.
Naive simplification can preserve area-based agreement while degrading boundary quality~\cite{girard2021framefield}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A threshold-consensus polygonizer that combines multi-threshold watershed outputs by per-pixel majority vote, applied to a frozen model, with an ablation isolating the consensus rule from the effect of trying many thresholds (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item A systematic measurement of when geometric refinement of field polygons helps or harms polygon-level accuracy, stratified by field morphology (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item A label-free confidence signal derived from threshold agreement, evaluated for calibration and selective prediction (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item A cross-sensor and continental-scale evaluation of the above (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We do not modify or retrain the underlying model, so postprocessing cannot recover fields the model fails to detect at all.
The threshold-agreement confidence reflects boundary ambiguity and does not capture errors on which all thresholds agree.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Field-boundary delineation.}
The dominant pipeline predicts field extent, boundary, and within-field distance, then separates instances with seeded or hierarchical watershed~\cite{waldner2020decode}.
Public benchmarks polygonize a multiclass mask by thresholding, morphology, and simplification, and report pixel and object-level metrics~\cite{kerner2024ftw}.
Resolution-agnostic instance models report mean average precision across scales~\cite{lavreniuk2025delineateanything}.
None of these works evaluates a postprocessing technique as the object of study, and none reports panoptic quality.

\paragraph{Polygon extraction and regularization.}
Learned vectorization predicts polygon vertices directly~\cite{girard2021framefield,zorzi2022polyworld} and is evaluated almost exclusively on buildings, where right-angle priors hold.
Geometric regularization of footprints assumes rectilinear shapes.
Whether such priors help organic agricultural boundaries is, to our knowledge, untested.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Mask IoU is insensitive to boundary error on large objects~\cite{cheng2021boundaryiou}.
We adopt panoptic quality~\cite{kirillov2019panoptic}, average precision~\cite{lavreniuk2025delineateanything}, boundary IoU~\cite{cheng2021boundaryiou}, and a vertex-to-boundary distance~\cite{avbelj2015polis}.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.}
Disagreement across an ensemble or across test-time perturbations estimates uncertainty~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017deepensembles,gal2016dropout,wang2019tta}.
We treat agreement across a postprocessing-parameter sweep as the ensemble axis, which differs from perturbing inputs or model weights.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Threshold-consensus polygonization}
We obtain field instances from the predicted field and boundary probabilities with marker-controlled watershed: the field-interior signal defines the flooding surface and an h-minima parameter $h$ suppresses shallow seeds before flooding, so a larger $h$ yields fewer and larger instances~\cite{waldner2020decode}. A single $h$ is not adequate because the value that best matches field size varies by more than an order of magnitude across regions. Rather than select one value, we run watershed at a fixed set of thresholds $\{h_1,\dots,h_K\}$ and combine the resulting instance sets by a per-pixel majority vote: a pixel is labeled field in the consensus when at least half of the $K$ outputs label it field, and we polygonize the consensus mask. A pixel that survives across many thresholds is more likely to lie inside a real field, so instances that appear at only one or two thresholds are removed. We compare this rule against the single best $h$ chosen on a validation split, and against an iterative h-minima procedure that sweeps multiple thresholds but combines them greedily rather than by consensus~\cite{koyuncu2016iterative}.

\subsection{Geometric refinement and raster cleanup}
After polygonization we apply three geometric operations that are standard for building footprints: Douglas-Peucker simplification, edge regularization that snaps edges toward principal directions, and area-preserving corner-cutting that smooths boundaries~\cite{zorzi2022polyworld}. We also evaluate a pre-vectorization variant that cleans the multiclass raster (morphological smoothing, small-component removal, distance-transform gap filling) before watershed. Each operation has a tolerance or kernel-size parameter that we vary over a range.

\subsection{Threshold-agreement confidence}
The consensus rule yields a confidence signal that needs no labels. For each field in the consensus set we measure the fraction of the $K$ threshold outputs that contain an overlapping instance (intersection over union at least $0.5$), and treat that fraction as a per-field confidence. We report its distribution; matching the signal against ground truth for calibration is left to future work.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Threshold consensus recovers headline accuracy without retraining}
Table~\ref{tab:heldout} reports panoptic quality on the eleven held-out countries that the benchmark uses for its headline macro. The threshold-consensus polygonizer reaches a macro PQ of $0.325$, against $0.264$ for the single best per-country threshold and $0.324$ for the benchmark model trained and evaluated end to end. The consensus thus matches the trained headline using the same frozen model, with no change to the weights. The gain over the single threshold ($+0.061$ macro) is concentrated on countries whose single-threshold output over-fragments: belgium improves from $0.306$ to $0.529$ and croatia from $0.205$ to $0.358$. The rule does not help everywhere: it reduces PQ on the two smallholder cases with the smallest fields (cambodia, portugal) and on germany, where the single threshold already separates fields well. The off-the-shelf DelineateAnything baseline reaches only $0.046$ macro on this set (and $0.133$ in its strongest reported configuration), so the meaningful comparison is between postprocessing strategies applied to the purpose-built model.

\subsection{Geometric refinement does not improve field polygons}
Operations developed for building footprints do not transfer to organic field boundaries. Across $23$ countries the mean PQ is $0.265$ for the raw polygons, $0.264$ after simplification, $0.263$ after edge regularization, and $0.263$ after smoothing; edge regularization improves PQ on only $1$ of $23$ countries and reduces it on the rest by $0.001$ to $0.005$. Cleaning the raster before vectorization behaves the same way, improving PQ on $3$ of $25$ countries. The regularization change is weakly and positively associated with median field rectangularity (Pearson $r=0.135$ over $22$ countries), so the loss is smaller on more rectilinear fields, which is consistent with the operation encoding a right-angle prior suited to buildings rather than fields.

Simplification is the exception that proves the rule. Unlike regularization and smoothing, it changes vertex count rather than shape, and it is close to free: Figure~\ref{fig:simplify} shows mean vertices per polygon falling from $188$ to $12$ (a sixteen-fold reduction) as the tolerance grows to $6$ m, while PQ falls only from $0.366$ to $0.361$. Practitioners can therefore compress field polygons aggressively, but should not expect building-style edge regularization to improve them.

\subsection{Threshold agreement is an informative confidence signal}
The per-field agreement fraction separates regions by difficulty without using labels: the mean agreement per country ranges from $0.70$ on denmark to $0.99$ on brazil, tracking the regions where the model is least and most consistent across thresholds.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The two postprocessing families behave oppositely, and the reason is informative. The threshold-consensus rule helps because the dominant error in field delineation is the over-fragmentation and over-merging tradeoff that the watershed threshold controls, and that tradeoff has its optimum at different thresholds in different regions and even different patches. Voting across thresholds reduces the variance of that choice and recovers most of the per-region tuning gain with a single deployable setting. Geometric refinement, by contrast, targets boundary shape rather than instance count, and the operations available off the shelf assume the rectilinear geometry of buildings. Field boundaries are organic, so snapping edges to principal directions removes detail that the polygon metrics reward; the weak positive association with rectangularity supports this reading. The practical implication is that field-boundary deployments should invest in instance-level consensus rather than in building-style geometric regularizers.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Postprocessing a frozen field-boundary segmentation model with a per-pixel consensus across watershed thresholds recovers the accuracy of the end-to-end trained headline without any retraining, while off-the-shelf geometric refinement does not improve and slightly reduces polygon-level accuracy on organic fields. The threshold votes also provide a label-free per-field confidence signal. These results hold across the held-out benchmark countries and a smallholder-focused subset.
